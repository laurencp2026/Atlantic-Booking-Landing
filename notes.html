01 / Problem discovery

Task 1: Think It Through Before You Build

Before building the product, I identified a possible customer, a specific problem and a simple solution to test. These are initial hypotheses rather than proven market findings.

My hunch before AI

“I think small surf-school owners would pay for a simple booking management tool because weather cancellations create extra admin work and can cause them to lose revenue when customers do not rebook.”

My chosen niche

Small surf schools in Ireland that frequently cancel lessons because of weather and sea conditions, and need a simpler way to rebook customers into alternative sessions.

Who might buy it?

The owner or manager of a small surf school. They may pay for a tool that reduces administration and helps recover bookings affected by cancellations.

Who uses it daily?

The owner, manager or booking administrator who handles cancelled sessions, contacts customers and arranges alternative lesson times.

What technology?

A mobile-friendly web application for recording cancellations, rebooking customers into sessions with available capacity, and tracking the value of bookings still awaiting rebooking.

The problem to investigate

When conditions are unsuitable for surfing, a school may need to reorganise multiple bookings quickly. I want to find out whether a dedicated rebooking workflow would make this process easier.

What might customers say?

How might schools handle this today?

Possible existing methods include paper diaries, phone calls and WhatsApp messages. These are assumptions to investigate, not verified facts about Atlantic Edge Surf School or every surf school in Ireland. Research and conversations with potential users would help establish what tools they actually use and where the biggest difficulties occur.

02 / Market research

Existing alternatives

I reviewed two established booking products to understand which parts of the booking-management job they already support.

Alternative 01

Bookwhen

Official website: bookwhen.com

What it supports: Class and event booking, session scheduling, attendee management and capacity-related booking workflows. These features overlap with parts of a surf school’s booking-management job.

Relevance to Atlantic Rebook: It shows that scheduling and managing bookings are already served by established tools. Atlantic Rebook would need to demonstrate a clear benefit for the specific process of handling weather cancellations and tracking bookings that still need to be rearranged.

03 / Commercial assumption

Business model

My initial pricing hypothesis is a subscription of €19 per month per surf school.

The assumption is that an owner would consider this price if the product saved enough administrative time or helped recover enough bookings to justify the monthly cost.

This price has not been validated with paying customers. To test it, I would show the prototype to surf-school owners, ask how they currently handle cancellations, and find out whether the proposed workflow solves a sufficiently important problem at this price.

04 / Product plan

Lean PRD

The first version should focus on one clear problem rather than trying to build a complete booking platform.

  1. Hypothesis A dedicated rebooking workflow can help small surf schools manage weather-related cancellations with less manual administration.
  2. Target audience Owners and managers of small surf schools in Ireland that are affected by weather and sea-condition cancellations.
  3. One main call to action “Register interest in trying Atlantic Rebook.”
  4. Success measure Count registrations of interest and measure how long it takes to organise rebookings during a test with potential users.
  5. Out of scope Online payments, automated customer messages, live weather forecasts and integrations with external booking systems.

05 / Why this project?

Project purpose

Atlantic Rebook is a prototype exploring how small surf schools could organise weather-cancelled lessons more easily. It focuses on three related tasks: seeing today's sessions, moving affected bookings to another session with available capacity, and understanding the value of bookings still awaiting rebooking.

The aim is to test whether this focused workflow is useful before investing in a fully functioning product. The project is designed around the operational realities of surf lessons, where conditions can change and staff may need to reorganise bookings at short notice.

06 / What it does not prove

Limitations

  • The demo uses fictional sample bookings and does not contain real customer information.
  • It is a prototype rather than a complete production booking system.
  • Data changes are stored locally in the browser for demonstration; there is no shared database for a real team.
  • It does not send real customer notifications or process payments.
  • It does not provide live weather forecasts or connect to external booking platforms.
  • Customer demand, willingness to pay and the estimated value of time saved have not yet been validated.
  • The competitor review is preliminary, and the possible current practices described above remain assumptions until researched.

AI assistance was used as a support tool rather than proof that the idea is commercially viable. I still need to check the links, test the interface, verify behaviour in the browser and validate the assumptions with potential users.

08 / Testing & iteration

Alpha test

I tested the landing page and demo to check that the main journey worked and that the prototype behaved as expected.

What I tried

I opened the landing page on my phone and clicked the “Try the demo” button to check whether it opened the Atlantic Rebook application.

One thing broken or confusing

The “Try the demo” button did not open the demo because the URL in the HTML code was incorrect.

What I changed

I edited the landing page's index.html and corrected the button's link to point to the published Atlantic Rebook demo.

Result

After correcting the URL, I clicked the button again and confirmed that it opened the published Atlantic Rebook demo successfully.

Additional feature checks

I tested the demo's cancellation, rebooking and money-at-risk views, checked the fictional sample records, and refreshed the page to see whether changes persisted.

What I learned: A working interface is not enough if users cannot reach it from the landing page. Testing the complete journey helped me identify and fix a broken link. The additional checks also helped me consider whether the main prototype workflows were understandable and whether changes remained after refreshing.
